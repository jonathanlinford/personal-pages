Two tracks, one search

What I want is not what a 5-year-old needs, so the shortlist splits cleanly.

For me · now

Classical guitar, adult

Cello for a decade, reads and sight-reads, choir ear. The natural jump is classical guitar (nylon string, read-music, fingerstyle technique) rather than a strum-along beginner track. I want a teacher who can skip the fundamentals.

Top pick: Sophie Stanley, BYU classical guitar professor. Backup: Hatch Guitar Studio (closest serious private studio) or a Thumbtack classical pro like Bradley B. Stone.
For my son · soon

Almost 5, first instrument

Here is the honest part: almost no studio in the corridor teaches guitar under 6, and the expert sweet spot is 7 to 9. The winning move is a music-readiness bridge now, then a small nylon guitar around 6 or 7.

Top pick: Let's Play Music (Spanish Fork, ages 4-6) as the bridge. If he must have guitar sooner: Mark Alger (Suzuki guitar) or Hatch at 6.

For my son (turning 5 in September)

The reality: guitar at 5 is the young edge. Expert consensus puts the sweet spot at 7 to 9. Best plan is a music-readiness bridge now, then a small nylon guitar around 6-7.

When is a kid ready for guitar?

From concert guitarist Douglas Niedt, Music Pandit, School of Rock, and Fender.

🎯 Age

Most teachers start around 7-9. Age 5 is the young edge only some specialists take. Readiness (focus, following direction, interest) matters more than the birthday.

🎸 The guitar

Use a nylon-string (softer on little fingers) in 1/2 or 3/4 size. Niedt: cheap guitars are "little more than toys," and do not buy one "too large, thinking they will grow into it."

⏱️ Practice

Short sessions of 2-4x the child's age in minutes, about 5 days a week, with a parent working alongside them.

🌉 The bridge

At 5, a music-readiness or piano-based program is the standard on-ramp (ear, rhythm, reading) until hands and attention are guitar-ready.
