02

Meet the prophet. Guess the child.

4 minutes

Isaiah lived in Jerusalem about 700 years before Jesus was born. He warned people when they were doing wrong, and he told them about the Savior who would come.

Where does he fit?

Remember Hezekiah and the frightening letter? Isaiah lived during that king’s lifetime. The Bible’s books are not all arranged as one straight timeline: we are returning to the time of Judah’s kings.

David & SolomonThe kingdom before Isaiah
IsaiahDuring Judah’s kings
Jesus ChristHundreds of years later
Listen before you guess

Who did Isaiah see coming?

Raise your hand when you know. Keep the name to yourself until all three clues are open.

  1. A child would be born.
  2. He would rule as a king.
  3. He would be called the Prince of Peace.

Jesus Christ.

03

One Savior. Many names.

6 minutes

One person can be a brother, a dad, and a teacher. Each title tells you something about him. Listen for the titles Isaiah gives the promised Savior.

“For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.”Isaiah 9:6
Tap a title · talk about what it means

What kind of Savior is He?

Which title would you remember?

You’re worried about tomorrow. Your friends want you to do something unkind. You feel sorry about a wrong choice.

More than one title can fit. Ask: “Why that one? What could you do next?”

If asked about “The everlasting Father”: Jesus is our Savior and gives us spiritual rebirth and everlasting life. Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ are distinct persons. See Mosiah 5:7. “Wonderful” reminds us of the wonder of who He is and what He does.

Read the other name in Isaiah 7:14; its meaning is in Matthew 1:23.

04

A stain is not the end of the story.

7 minutes

Imagine spilling red juice on a white shirt. What would you do? What if you thought the stain could never come out?

Isaiah uses bright colors to teach a promise. Scarlet and crimson mean red. Snow and wool give us a picture of becoming clean.

“Though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow.”Isaiah 1:18
A picture of the promise

What can Jesus do?

Scarlet

First ask: “Is the Lord telling us to give up, or inviting us to come back?”

The changing color is a symbol. Jesus Christ makes forgiveness possible as we repent and turn to Him.

“Cease to do evil; learn to do well.”Isaiah 1:16–17

A child borrows a friend’s toy without asking, breaks it, and hides it. Build a better ending together. Tap each next step after the children suggest it.

Make it right

What might each step sound like out loud?

What if the friend is still upset?

We can keep being honest, make repairs where possible, and give other people time. Forgiveness does not instantly undo every consequence.

You are loved while you are learning to change. Jesus helps you come back.

05

“Here am I; send me.”

5 minutes

Isaiah had a vision of the Lord. He felt unworthy. Before we hear what Isaiah volunteered to do, notice what the Lord did for him.

Isaiah 6:1–8 · three moments
1 / 3“I am a man of unclean lips.”

Isaiah sees the Lord and feels his own imperfections. He does not pretend he has none.

“Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then said I, Here am I; send me.”Isaiah 6:8
Notice the order

God helped Isaiah become clean, then Isaiah offered to serve. How might the Lord help someone who wants to do good but feels nervous or imperfect?

Stand and volunteer. Everyone who wants to may stand, or raise a hand while seated. Read: “A new child needs a seat.” “The room needs cleaning.” “Someone needs a friend.” After each one, say together, “Here am I; send me,” and let one child name something helpful they could actually do.

Isaiah was called as a prophet. We can be willing to do the work God gives us, too.

06

Try being the peacemaker.

5 minutes

If we follow the Prince of Peace, what could change at recess tomorrow?

Choose your words

Talk together before choosing.

Act it out. Two volunteers both want the same seat. Give them thirty seconds to prepare a peaceful ending, then try it. What words helped? Can you explain what you need and still be kind?

If the argument continues, step away and ask an adult for help. Making peace does not mean accepting someone hurting you.

07

Come and learn His ways.

3 minutes

Isaiah also saw people from many nations coming to the Lord’s house. In the temple we learn about Jesus Christ and make sacred promises with God.

“He will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths.”Isaiah 2:3
Two halves of one verse

What does the Lord do? Teach us. What do we do? Walk in His paths.

Try the motions. Hold your hands like an open book for “teach us.” Move two fingers along your other palm for “walk in his paths.” Say the line twice, then try it without looking.

Ask: “What is one thing you can learn about Jesus now that will help you prepare for the temple?”

Optional song, if time allows: “I Love to See the Temple,” Children’s Songbook, 95.

08

One small “here am I.”

2 minutes

Choose a name and let that child finish the sentence. Anyone may pass. With only two minutes, invite two or three answers.

Choose a sentence starter.
“Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and not be afraid.”Isaiah 12:2
This week

Pick one person. Do one good thing.

Include someone. Make something right. Help without being asked. Choose one thing you can actually do this week.

Share your own brief testimony of the Savior’s forgiveness, guidance, and peace. Close with prayer.

Teacher notes, sources & earlier lessons

Eight steps total about 35 minutes, including prayers and the short video in step 2. The matching game and song are extra. For a 20-minute class: review (2), titles (5), repentance (6), Isaiah’s call (4), and take-home (3). No printing, purchases, or supplies needed.

  • Have children answer before tapping. Invite them to read the short passages aloud.
  • Keep repentance examples imaginary. Do not ask children to confess personal sins in class.
  • The class pictures are newly generated, imagined examples based on the children’s photo references, not claims about events in their lives. Isaiah’s picture is an original generated illustration, not a historical photograph.
  • In Isaiah’s vision, the coal touching his lips represents cleansing. Tell the story; do not act out that part with heat or objects.
  • The review looks back to the available Psalms pages, without assuming those were last Sunday’s lesson.
  • Sound is optional and starts off. Pictures open full screen. Games restart without reloading.
