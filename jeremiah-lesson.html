Gather with love.

5 minutes

Read Jeremiah 16:14–16. Ask the children to mime casting a fishing line.

Jeremiah described fishers and hunters finding people. Think of the care and effort it takes to find someone. We can help people come closer to Jesus by loving, serving, and inviting them.

People are never prizes to catch. We respect their choices and keep being their friends.

Love people before, during, and after an invitation.

05

In the potter’s hands.

6 minutes

Read Jeremiah 18:1–6. Point back to the workshop picture. “Marred” means spoiled or damaged. What did the potter do next?

The potter did not give up when the vessel lost its shape. He worked the clay into another vessel. The Lord used this picture to teach Israel about His power to shape His people.

“Behold, as the clay is in the potter’s hand, so are ye in mine hand.”Jeremiah 18:6

Teacher notes, sources & timing

The six sections total 27 minutes. Reserve three minutes for closing prayer, transitions, and extra answers. The opening prayer fits within the review. No supplies or printing are needed. Optional clay can replace the screen demonstration.

  • Before class: check the embedded video’s playback. The official story and YouTube links are available below it if needed.
  • The illustrations replace extra spoken examples within the listed times. Ask one picture question per section. If time runs short, use one scripture-detective card, one gathering scenario, and two pottery stages. Keep the final invitation.
  • The class pictures are newly generated, imagined examples based on the children’s earlier lesson illustrations—not claims about events in their lives. Invite voluntary answers; children may pass. Tap a picture to enlarge it.
  • God’s love does not depend on performance. The clay comparison is about learning and repentance; people have agency, feelings, and worth.
  • The Lord’s promise to Jeremiah did not prevent opposition. Avoid promising that faithful choices always make life easy.
