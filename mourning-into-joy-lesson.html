Sunday, November 1 · Primary, age 9 · 30 minutes

God’s love is everlasting. We can turn to Jesus Christ in happy times and hard times.

1

Remember what we learned

3 minutes

Begin with prayer. Take one or two answers to these review questions.

  • What did the Lord tell Jeremiah before calling him to be a prophet?
  • At the potter’s house, what happened when the clay vessel lost its shape?
Review answers

The Lord knew Jeremiah before he was born and promised to help him. The potter shaped the clay again. God knows us and can help us grow.

Today we will learn what the Lord taught His people when they faced difficult times. Some had turned away from Him, but He still invited them to return and offered love and hope.

2

God loves me with an everlasting love

4 minutes

Read together: Jeremiah 31:3

“I have loved thee with an everlasting love.”Jeremiah 31:3

Everlasting means that God’s love does not run out. We are His children when we succeed, when we struggle, and when we need to repent.

A simple comparison

Name something that does not last forever: an ice cube, a snack, or a birthday candle. Then ask: “Does Heavenly Father’s love run out like those things?”

Invite the children to hold their hands apart as though showing something very long. Explain that even a very long distance has an end, but God’s love has no end.

  • How can we remember God’s love when we feel lonely or make a mistake?
  • Who helps you feel Heavenly Father’s love? How could you help someone else feel it?

Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ love me, and I can always turn to Them.

3

I can keep God’s teachings in my heart

6 minutes

Read together: Jeremiah 31:33

“Write it in their hearts.”Jeremiah 31:33

The Lord promised to make a covenant with His people. A covenant is a sacred agreement with God. Having His teachings written in our hearts means learning to love Him and choosing to follow Him.

Connect this to baptism. Read or summarize Mosiah 18:8–10: we are willing to serve God, keep His commandments, comfort others, and stand as witnesses of Him. He promises His Spirit. Children preparing for baptism can practice these things, too.

What would that look like?

Invite everyone to trace a heart in the air. Then discuss two of these situations. Let the children answer before opening the suggested response.

A child is sitting alone. How could I help?

Invite them to sit with you, make room, and listen to them. Respect their choice and continue being kind.

I said something unkind. What can I do next?

Ask Heavenly Father for help, apologize, and do what you can to make things right. Jesus Christ helps us repent and change.

I can tell the truth even though nobody saw what happened.

Choose honesty because you want to follow Jesus. We can do what is right without needing an audience or a reward.

  • What is the difference between knowing a commandment and choosing to live it?
  • How can Jesus help us when keeping a promise is difficult?

Jesus Christ helps me keep my covenants and become more like Him.

4

The scriptures help me turn to God

5 minutes

Read together: Jeremiah 36:2–4

Jeremiah, Baruch, and the scroll

The Lord told Jeremiah to record His words so the people could hear, turn away from evil, and receive forgiveness. Jeremiah spoke, and Baruch wrote the words on a scroll. Baruch then read them to the people.

Later, King Jehoiakim had the scroll read to him. He cut it and burned it instead of listening. The Lord told Jeremiah to have the words written again, and Baruch wrote another scroll.

Story references: Jeremiah 36:4–10, 21–25, 27–28, 32.

Listen for a reason

Read verse 3 again. Ask the children to listen for why the Lord wanted the people to hear His words.

What did the Lord want for His people?

He wanted them to turn away from wrongdoing so He could forgive them. His warnings were an invitation to change.

  • Does ignoring a true message make it stop being true?
  • What is one way the scriptures can help you make a choice this week?

If there is time, invite one child to name a favorite scripture or scripture story. Emphasize responding to God’s word with a willing heart. No supplies or acting out the burning are needed.

I can listen to the Lord’s words and act on what I learn.

5

I can stand up for what is right

6 minutes

Read together: Jeremiah 38:7–13

Ebed-melech helps Jeremiah

Some officials put Jeremiah into a muddy pit because they opposed his message. Ebed-melech, an Ethiopian man who served in the king’s household, heard what had happened and spoke to the king about it.

The king allowed him to take helpers to rescue Jeremiah. Ebed-melech brought ropes and old cloth. He told Jeremiah to place the cloth under his arms to cushion the ropes. Then the men pulled Jeremiah out. Jeremiah remained in the prison courtyard.

  • What did Ebed-melech do that was brave?
  • Why do you think he took the time to find soft cloth?
  • How can we be both courageous and kind?

Practice one kind sentence

Imagine a child is being left out. Ask two volunteers to practice what they could say, such as “Would you like to join us?” Then ask what they could do to make the invitation real.

If someone is being bullied or is in danger, getting a trusted adult’s help is a brave choice.

Anyone may pass or answer from their seat. Keep the rescue as a story; do not tie, pull, or lift children. Courage includes seeking help.

I can speak up for what is right and help others with kindness.

6

Jesus Christ gives me hope

4 minutes

Lamentations contains poems of sorrow after Jerusalem and its temple were destroyed. In the middle of that sadness, we find words about the Lord’s mercy.

Read together: Lamentations 3:22–26

“They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”Lamentations 3:23

“They” means the Lord’s mercies—His loving care. We can ask for His help again and again. Through Jesus Christ, we can be forgiven, receive comfort, and look forward to resurrection and lasting joy.

  • What do these verses teach us about the Lord when life is hard?
  • Who could you turn to when you need comfort or help?

Children can think quietly rather than share a sad experience. Explain that sadness does not mean they have done something wrong. God’s love continues while problems take time to work through.

I can feel sad and still have hope in Jesus Christ.

7

Choose one thing to do this week

2 minutes

Invite each child to choose one way to follow Jesus this week:

  • Remember God’s love and pray for His help.
  • Read a scripture and act on one thing it teaches.
  • Include someone or stand up kindly for what is right.

Invite one or two children to finish: “Today I learned that…” Bear a brief testimony of Heavenly Father’s love and the hope we have through Jesus Christ. Close with prayer.

Heavenly Father loves me. Jesus Christ helps me choose the right and begin again.

Teacher notes & sources

The seven sections total 30 minutes, including prayers and transitions. No supplies are required. If time is short, use one covenant scenario and summarize the scroll story. Keep the closing invitation.
