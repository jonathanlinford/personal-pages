The Lord Is My Shepherd

Psalms 1–2; 8; 19–33; 40; 46 · August 23, 2026

Every other week this year we have read a story. This week there is no story. This week is the songbook, and the most famous song in it was written by a boy who used to smell like sheep.

A king could have written about thrones or armies. He wrote about sheep.

01

Last week, quickly

~2 min

Warm-up: Two questions about Job. Tap the one you think is right.

1. God finally answered Job out of the whirlwind. What did the answer sound like?
Questions. Not one explanation in the whole speech. God did not tell Job why. He showed Job who was holding the world together.
2. Job said one famous line while he still had nothing and still had no answer. Which one?
He said it in the dark. Before the ending. Before anything got better.
Bridge into today
Job trusted God without getting an explanation. Fine. But what do you actually say to God on a day like that? That is what this whole book is. Psalms is 150 sets of words for people who need some.
02

A book with no story in it

~4 min

Psalms is the Bible's songbook. One hundred fifty songs, collected over hundreds of years. They were written to be sung out loud with instruments, and the tunes are all lost. We only have the words.

Ask the class: what is a song you know by heart? Why do people sing when they are happy, and also sing when they are sad?

Tap a line. Guess what the person writing it was feeling.

Abandoned. Guilty. Rescued. So happy you cannot sit down. All four are in the same book, and sometimes on the same page.

That is the point worth landing. God is not only interested in your good days. There is a psalm for the bad ones and He kept those too.

03

The shepherd boy grew up

~5 min

You already know this kid. Back in June we met him: the youngest of eight brothers, out with the sheep while everybody else got looked at, the one the Lord picked because the Lord looketh on the heart. Then the sling, then the giant.

Before any of that, his job was keeping sheep alive. Finding water. Finding grass. Chasing off a lion and a bear. Carrying the hurt ones. Counting them every single night.

Then he became king of the whole nation. And when King David sat down to write about God, out of every picture he could have used, he reached for the one job he knew best.

Ask them
If David is the shepherd, and God is David's shepherd, then what does that make David? A sheep. And he is completely fine with that. He is not embarrassed about it. He wrote it down and let people sing it for three thousand years.
📖 Read together
Psalm 23:1

"The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want."

🔍 One word

Not "a shepherd." My shepherd. A shepherd with a whole flock still knows which one is yours.

04

Psalm 23, one line at a time

~9 min

Six verses. Tap each line and walk the psalm end to end. Do not rush verse four.

1
"The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want."
Psalm 23:1
"I shall not want" does not mean you get everything you ask for. It means there is nothing you are missing.
2
"He maketh me to lie down in green pastures."
Psalm 23:2
A scared sheep will not lie down. It stands there watching. So a sheep lying down in the grass is a sheep that feels safe.
3
"He leadeth me beside the still waters."
Psalm 23:2
Sheep will not drink from fast water. It scares them. The shepherd's job is to go find the calm spot first.
4
"He restoreth my soul."
Psalm 23:3
Ask them: what do you do with a sheep that is worn out? You do not yell at it and you do not make it walk faster. You let it rest.
5
"Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me."
Psalm 23:4 · stop here
Read it again slowly. He does not say the shepherd takes him around the valley. He says through. Nobody is promised there will not be a dark valley. What you are promised is who is walking in it next to you.
6
"Thy rod and thy staff they comfort me."
Psalm 23:4
Two different tools. The rod is a club, for whatever is out there hunting the sheep. The staff is the long one with the hook, for pulling a stuck sheep out of a hole. Ask which one they have needed lately.
7
"Thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over."
Psalm 23:5
Not "my cup is full." Full is enough. Running over is spilling down the sides onto the table.
8
"And I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever."
Psalm 23:6
The psalm starts in a field and ends in the house of the Lord. And the last word of the whole thing is forever.
Eight lines. Tap the first one.

Six verses, and four of them are about being taken care of by somebody who knows what he is doing. One is about a dark valley. And the last word is forever.

David wrote this after he had been hunted, betrayed by his own son, and had wrecked things badly himself. He is not saying life is gentle. He is saying he knows who is walking with him.

05

The shepherd's voice

~5 min

Sheep are not smart, but they are very good at one thing: they know their own shepherd's voice. A stranger can stand in the same field and call them and they will not move.

▶ Do this in the room

Find the shepherd

Nothing to bring. This works with an empty classroom and six kids.

How it runs

  • Pick one kid to be the shepherd. Send them to the far side of the room.
  • Everybody else is a sheep. Eyes closed, no peeking.
  • The shepherd calls the sheep by name, quietly, one at a time. Each sheep walks toward the voice with eyes still shut.
  • Round two: pick a second kid to call at the same time, using a silly loud voice. The sheep have to keep following the right one.

Then ask: how did you know which voice to follow? Not because it was the loudest. Because you already knew it. That is the whole reason we practice hearing the Spirit on ordinary days, so we can pick it out on the loud ones.

"The Lord Is My Shepherd," Hymn 108

06

The sky is talking

~4 min

David spent a lot of nights outside with nothing to look at but the sky. Two of his psalms come straight out of that.

📖 Read together
Psalm 19:1

"The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork."

Psalm 8:3–4

"When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars... what is man, that thou art mindful of him?"

Go around the class
One thing each. What is something God made that makes you think He is really there? Take every answer, including the funny ones.

Then make the turn, because David does. He looks at all of that and asks the honest question: if you built that, why would you bother remembering me? He does not answer it in Psalm 8. He answers it in Psalm 23. The shepherd counts every single sheep, every single night, and notices when one is missing.

07

Clean hands and a pure heart

~3 min

Psalm 24 asks a question you would ask outside a temple, because that is probably where people sang it.

📖 Read together
Psalm 24:3–4

"Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? or who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart."

Have everybody hold up their hands and look at them. Then the important part, said out loud, because 9-year-olds get this one wrong in a way that follows them for years: a pure heart does not mean never messing up. Nobody in the room qualifies on that plan, and nobody ever has.

Here is the proof, from the same songbook, by the same guy. David wrote Psalm 32 after he did something genuinely terrible.

📖 The one to notice
Psalm 32:5

"I acknowledged my sin unto thee... and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin."

🔍 Focus

The shepherd does not write off the sheep that wandered. He goes and gets it. Every time.

08

Be still

~3 min

One more line, six words long, and it is an instruction rather than a description.

📖 Read together
Psalm 46:10

"Be still, and know that I am God."

Everybody completely still and completely silent until the circle turns gold. No talking, no fidgeting, no laughing. Hardest thirty seconds of Primary.

30

Ask what they noticed. Somebody will say "it was so long," and somebody will say they heard something they had not heard before. Both are the right answer.

God almost never shouts over the noise. Sometimes the stillness is not the warm-up. It is the whole instruction.

09

Take it home

~2 min

Nobody wrote these psalms as an assignment. Somebody was scared, or sorry, or so relieved they could not sit down, and they reached for words. Then other people kept the words because they needed them too.

🔍 One thing to try this week

Write your own psalm. One line to God about something real, good or hard. It does not have to rhyme, nobody has to read it, and you are allowed to complain in it. David did, constantly.

🎵 Song
The Lord Is My Light

Children's Songbook 62, straight out of Psalm 27:1. Or Hymn 108 if you want the shepherd one.

If you only get one sentence in
The Lord is a shepherd who knows which sheep is yours, walks with you through the dark part instead of steering around it, and goes back for the one that wandered off.

One more thing

Say It in Order 🐑

All eight lines of Psalm 23, shuffled. Tap them in order, from the beginning. Get one wrong and it shakes at you and you try again. Fewer mistakes is better.

Teacher notes

  • Total runs about 37 minutes with all nine steps. If you are short, cut step 6 (the sky) and keep the valley and the stillness.
  • Nothing to print, buy, or carry in. The only two activities need an empty room and a class that can shut its eyes.
  • Put the stillness late, after they have moved. It does not work cold.
  • Do not try to get all six verses memorized. Psalm 23:1 and Psalm 46:10 are the two worth repeating out loud.
  • Say the "pure heart does not mean never messing up" line even if you are running behind. That is the one somebody in the room needs.
