Come, Follow Me · October 5–11, 2026

He hath borne our griefs,
and carried our sorrows.

Isaiah saw that the Savior would not rescue us from far away. Jesus would come close, carry what is heavy, and lead us in a higher way.

Isaiah 50–57   /   Sunday, October 11   /   Primary, age 9   /   30 minutes

Jesus Christ understands my pain, carries my burdens, and shows me a higher way.

01

Remember the marvellous work.

3 minutes

Open with prayer. Let the children answer before tapping.

Two quick callbacks

What was last lesson’s important word?

Isaiah said Jesus is like what during a storm?

Last time Isaiah showed what Jesus can restore. Today he shows how close Jesus comes when life feels heavy.

02

“Awake … put on thy strength.”

4 minutes

Isaiah spoke to people who felt defeated and stuck. The Lord did not tell them to stay down. He invited them to wake up, stand, and use the strength He gives.

“Awake, awake; put on thy strength … shake thyself from the dust; arise.”Isaiah 52:1–2
Stand and move

Do Isaiah’s three motions.

Tap each motion, then do it together.

Ask the class

What kind of strength can Jesus give that muscles cannot?

03

Jesus carries what is heavy.

9 minutes

Pretend to hold a very heavy backpack for ten seconds. What happens when somebody reaches over and helps support it?

“Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows.”Isaiah 53:4–5

Grief is deep sadness. Sorrow is pain in the heart. Jesus experienced pain, temptation, loneliness, sickness, and every consequence of sin. He understands perfectly—not by guessing, but by experience.

Choose a burden. Carrying does not always mean making it disappear immediately.

Watch the 5:25 Church video. Before it begins, ask the children to listen for ways Jesus carried grief and sorrow. Afterward, take one or two answers.

Jesus was “wounded for our transgressions.” Because of Him, sin and death do not get the final word.

04

Seek the Lord.

4 minutes
“Seek ye the Lord while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near.”Isaiah 55:6

We do not seek Jesus because He is playing a hiding game. We seek Him by turning our attention and choices toward Him.

Where can I seek Him?

Open each doorway.

Choose one way you can seek Jesus this week.

05

His ways are higher.

5 minutes
“As the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways.”Isaiah 55:8–9
From inside a maze
“I cannot see the way.”
→
From above the maze
the whole path is visible.

God sees what we cannot see. His “higher way” is also a better way to treat people.

Someone makes a mistake. Which is Christ’s higher way?

You are chosen to lead. Which is Christ’s higher way?

Jesus’s higher way is merciful, honest, humble, and brave.

06

Give Him one burden.

2 minutes

Choose two or three names. Anyone may pass.

Choose a sentence starter.
This week

Name it. Give it. Take one step.

Tell Jesus what feels heavy. Ask Him to carry it with you. Then take one small step in His higher way.

Share a brief testimony that Jesus Christ knows each child perfectly and never asks them to carry life alone. Close with prayer.

Teacher notes, sources & timing

The six steps total 27 minutes; use the remaining three minutes for opening and closing prayers and transitions. If discussion runs long, shorten section 5 to one scenario. No printing, purchases, or supplies are needed.

  • Core path: review 3, awake 4, Isaiah 53 and video 9, seek 4, higher ways 5, closing 2.
  • Do not invite children to disclose private grief, trauma, or sin. Keep examples general and let anyone pass.
  • Do not promise that faith makes pain disappear immediately. Emphasize that Christ understands, strengthens, forgives, heals, and stays near.
  • The class pictures are newly generated, imagined examples based on the children’s reference images—not claims about events in their lives.
  • The official children’s outline suggests the “awake/stand/strength” motions, a heavy-object comparison for burdens, seeking the Lord, and viewing a situation from higher ground.
