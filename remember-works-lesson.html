I will remember.

Help the children remember what the Lord has done, return to Jesus Christ, and tell someone what they know.

Psalms 49-51; 61-66; 69-72; 77-78; 85-86 | Primary | about 36 minutes

01

Remember

9 minutes

Last week, the valley stayed dark, but the Shepherd stayed with us. This week, help the children think about how they can remember Him when another hard thing comes.

Start here

Psalms 77 and 78 belong together. In Psalm 77, one person is going through a terrible night and trying to remember whether God is still there. In Psalm 78, a whole people teach their children to tell the old rescue stories again.

The Israelites told the stories of the Red Sea, the manna, and the wilderness again and again. Forgetting was more than losing a fact. When they forgot what God had done, they stopped trusting what He could do next.

Source for the count and method: BYU Religious Studies Center

How did God help?

Remembering how God helped before can give us courage when another problem comes.

Tap a story.
After all six, ask
  • Which story would you want to remember on a scary day?
  • What does that story teach you about God?
  • How can remembering a rescue from long ago help when a new problem comes?

The goal is trust, not getting every Bible question right. Remembering what God has done helps us put our hope in Him.

02

The turn

6 minutes

Psalm 77 begins with someone who cannot sleep because he is too upset to be comforted. Scripture includes hard questions too.

Who is Asaph?

Asaph wrote the psalm and was a temple musician. His job was to help other people worship, yet he still had a night when prayer seemed unanswered. He remembered earlier songs and happier days, and that made the present hurt even more.

He asked questions frightened people still ask: "Has God stopped loving me? Has mercy run out? Did He forget His promise?" The psalm does not scold him for asking.

“Thou holdest mine eyes waking: I am so troubled that I cannot speak.”Psalm 77:4
“Hath God forgotten to be gracious? hath he in anger shut up his tender mercies?”Psalm 77:9
Before remembering"Has God forgotten me?"
→
What changes"I will remember."

At verse 11, nothing outside Asaph has changed. He is still awake, and the trouble is still real. He changes what he chooses to think about. Instead of predicting the future from one terrible night, he looks back at everything God has done.

“I will meditate also of all thy work, and talk of thy doings.”Psalm 77:12
“Thy way is in the sea … and thy footsteps are not known.”Psalm 77:19
Ask the class
  • What changed, Asaph's problem or what he chose to think about?
  • How can God's path be real when we cannot see His footprints?

Asaph is honest about the hard night. Then he remembers that the night is not the whole story.

03

Return

9 minutes

David made a choice he could not undo or excuse. He came to God and asked for help changing.

What is happening in Psalm 51?

After David was caught, he finally saw the harm he had caused and came to God without making excuses. Listen for the verbs he uses: wash me, cleanse me, create in me, renew me, restore me. David can confess and turn back, but he knows he cannot give himself a new heart.

Repentance gives us hope because Jesus Christ can clean, heal, and restore what we cannot fix alone.

“Wash me throughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin.”Psalm 51:2
“Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.”Psalm 51:7
“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”Psalm 51:10
Heart activity

Tap each choice that closes the heart

Repentance helps us come back to Jesus.

Jesus Christ can make a heart clean again.

04

Tell

8 minutes

A testimony does not have to explain everything. One true sentence about what Jesus has done for you can be a testimony.

Two ways to share

Psalm 66 says, “Come and see” what God has done in history. A few verses later it says, “Come and hear” what He has done for one person. A testimony can include both: what God has done in scripture and what you have seen Him do in your own life.

The psalmist does not claim to know everything about God. He shares the part he knows. A child can do that too.

“Come and see the works of God: he is terrible in his doing toward the children of men.”Psalm 66:5
“Come and hear … and I will declare what he hath done for my soul.”Psalm 66:16
“My mouth shall shew forth thy righteousness and thy salvation all the day.”Psalm 71:15

In Psalm 71, testimony continues through a whole life: “O God, thou hast taught me from my youth.” Even when the writer is old, he wants to tell the next generation about God's strength. For the next generation to hear, somebody has to speak.

One sentence each

Choose a sentence starter

Tap a child's name and read the sentence that appears. Let the child finish it with one short, honest answer. Anyone may pass. Go first so they can hear an example.

Choose a name.
Before children answer
  • Keep it simple and honest.
  • Help them be specific. They do not need to sound impressive.
  • "I prayed and felt calmer" is a complete testimony.

You do not need every answer first. Tell one true thing Jesus Christ has done for you.

05

Take it home

4 minutes
A pattern for prayer

David begins with “Bow down thine ear, O Lord, hear me.” He tells God about the trouble, asks plainly for help, remembers that God is “ready to forgive,” and asks God to teach him how to walk. His prayer asks God to help with the situation and to change him as he walks through it.

Teacher“In the day of my trouble…”
Children“I will call upon thee.”
“For thou wilt answer me.”Psalm 86:7
“Teach me thy way, O Lord; I will walk in thy truth.”Psalm 86:11

Try the call and response quietly. Repeat it as if everyone believes the words. Then try it once from memory.

This week

Tell one person what you remember

Choose one thing God did in scripture or in your life. Tell one person about it. It can take just ten seconds.

Close by saying, "Remember what God has done, and come back to Him when you wander. Then tell somebody what you know."

Teacher notes
  • Plan for about 36 minutes. If time is short, keep the memory wall, the change in Psalm 77, the clean-heart activity, and the one-sentence testimonies.
  • You do not need to print, buy, or bring anything.
  • The seven class pictures are examples. They do not claim that these events happened. Use them to ask what each principle might look like during an ordinary week.
  • Do not ask children to name specific sins. Keep the discussion of repentance centered on Christ and the next honest step.
  • Tomorrow is the fifth Sunday. The official Primary note encourages using Appendix B. This lesson supports the same goals with activities about remembering, repentance, prayer, and testimony.
