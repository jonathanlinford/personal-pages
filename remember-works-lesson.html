I Will Remember the Works of the Lord

When everything feels loud, dark, or confusing, the psalmist does one small thing: he remembers. Then he tells somebody else.

Psalms 49–51; 61–66; 69–72; 77–78; 85–86 · Primary · about 34 minutes

01About 3 minutes

Last week, in two questions

Start with the song everybody remembers. Let them choose before you reveal the answer.

Psalm 23 begins, “The Lord is my…”

A shepherd. David knew sheep, so that was the picture he chose for God.

What changes in the valley of the shadow of death?

The valley is still dark. The promise is “thou art with me.”
Bridge into today: Last week was about who walks with us. This week asks how we remember that when the next hard thing arrives.
02About 5 minutes

Six things worth remembering

Psalm 78 retells the old stories so the children will “set their hope in God.” Tap each memory from our year together.

Memory wall

What did the Lord do?

Choose a memory.
“I will remember the works of the Lord: surely I will remember thy wonders of old.”Psalm 77:11
Ask: Why keep telling children stories they have heard before? A remembered rescue becomes courage for the next problem.
03About 4 minutes

The verse where the psalm turns

Psalm 77 does not begin cheerful. The writer cries all night, cannot sleep, and asks whether God has forgotten to be kind. Scripture keeps the hard questions too.

Before“My soul refused to be comforted.”
The hinge“I will remember.”
AfterHe retells the Red Sea and sees a path again.

Nothing outside changes between verse 10 and verse 11. He is still awake. The problem is still there. But he chooses which evidence to look at.

“Thy way is in the sea, and thy path in the great waters, and thy footsteps are not known.”Psalm 77:19
Land this: Sometimes God is making a path and nobody can see His footprints yet.
04About 7 minutes

Create in me a clean heart

David wrote Psalm 51 after a choice he could not undo or excuse. He did not ask God to pretend it was fine. He asked God to change him.

“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”Psalm 51:10
Tap each closed place

What does repentance open?

These are not four punishments. They are four ways Jesus helps us come back.

A clean heart does not mean a heart that never needed cleaning. It means a heart that let Jesus Christ in.
“For thou, Lord, art good, and ready to forgive; and plenteous in mercy.”Psalm 86:5
Do it with their hands: Make two tight fists: hiding, defending, holding on. Then open both hands. A contrite heart is not smashed. It is open.
05About 4 minutes

Lead me to the rock

The psalmist does not claim to feel strong. He says his heart is overwhelmed and asks to be led somewhere stronger than he is.

“When my heart is overwhelmed: lead me to the rock that is higher than I.”Psalm 61:2–3

06About 5 minutes

Remember, then tell

Psalm 66 turns a memory into a testimony. The invitation is not “come hear everything I know.” It is smaller and more personal: come hear what He did for my soul.

“Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul.”Psalm 66:16
Pass the testimony

Give each person one doorway

Tap a name. Let them finish the sentence if they want; passing is allowed.

Choose a doorway.
Teacher goes first: Give one true sentence, not a sermon. Specific beats impressive.
07About 3 minutes

Call upon me

📞

A phone is only useful if someone answers

Prayer is not pretending God is nearby. It is calling because He really is.

“In the day of my trouble I will call upon thee: for thou wilt answer me.”Psalm 86:7

Call and response: Teacher says, “In the day of my trouble…” Class answers, “I will call upon thee.” Teacher: “Why?” Class: “For thou wilt answer me.” Try it quietly, then like they believe it, then from memory.

08About 3 minutes

Take one memory home

Go around once. Each child chooses one thing God did in scripture or in their life that they do not want to forget.

This week: Tell that one story to one person. It can take ten seconds: “I remembered this today, and it helped me trust Jesus.”

The psalmist moved from “Has God forgotten me?” to “I will remember.” Then from remembering to telling. That is the whole lesson.

One more thing

Put the memories in order

Six moments from this year are shuffled. Tap them from earliest to latest. A wrong answer shakes and stays in the bank.

Start with the sea.

Teacher notes

  • About 34 minutes with all eight steps. Short on time? Keep the memory wall, clean-heart activity, and testimony prompts.
  • Nothing to print or carry. The phone is a picture on the page, not an object you need to bring.
  • Tomorrow is a fifth Sunday. The official Primary note encourages Appendix B activities; this plan supports its covenant-path aim through scripture memory, repentance, prayer, and testimony.
  • Do not ask children to name sins. Keep repentance focused on Christ’s readiness to forgive and the next honest step.
  • When testimony prompts get personal, passing is always allowed. Go first with something brief and true.
