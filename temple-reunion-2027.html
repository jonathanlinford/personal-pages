The idea

One rare event. The whole family. A few days that feel like a holiday.

Next year the Salt Lake Temple opens its doors to everyone for six months. It is free, it is open to anyone regardless of faith or membership, and it will very likely never happen again in our lifetimes. That is the kind of thing worth flying people in for. So the plan is simple: everybody comes to Utah, we walk through the temple together on one special morning, and we build a proper family reunion around it. Below are three ways to do it, with real places to stay and the dates that make each one work.

Good to know, for everyone coming. The open house is open to all. You do not have to be a member, and there is no ticket cost. You walk the actual inside of the temple at your own pace, guided, in about 60 to 90 minutes. Dress is just respectful and comfortable, nothing special required, and kids are welcome. It is a genuinely rare thing to get to see, which is exactly why it makes such an easy, no-pressure reason to bring everybody together.

Plan A

Lake Days at Bear Lake

The "Caribbean of the Rockies," turquoise water, sandy beaches, and the world's most famous raspberry shakes. Big-family energy, kids in the water all day. Do the temple day on the drive up or back, since the temple sits right on the route.

Plan B · easiest logistics

Base Camp Midway

One house, one basecamp, no repacking. Heber Valley is a green alpine bowl 45 minutes from Temple Square, so the temple is a relaxed morning drive and you are back for lunch. This is the option if you want the least driving and the most "everyone under one roof."

Plan C · the road trip

Red Rock Reunion

Make it a proper Utah road trip. Spend a night or two near Salt Lake for the temple and Temple Square, then caravan south into red rock country for warm days, resort pools, and Zion. Best in spring or fall, since St. George bakes in midsummer.
