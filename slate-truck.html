What June 24 actually does to your deposit

Right now you are holding a reservation. On June 24 Slate reveals the final price, opens its order books, and asks reservation holders to convert into a preorder. That is the moment the money stops being yours to get back.

What you have today
$50

A reservation. Fully refundable. Holds your place in line and your priority timing. Walk away any time, get it back.

→
What June 24 asks for
$300

A preorder deposit, non-refundable. Your $50 carries over, so it is another $250 to convert. You get roughly 30 days to decide, and it applies to the purchase price.

The price is revealed the same day the deposit turns non-refundable. Expect the mid-$20,000s, not the under-$20,000 number that first got everyone excited. More on why below.
The vehicle

A truck stripped back to the studs, on purpose

Slate calls it the "Blank Slate." There is no paint, just gray composite panels you can wrap yourself. No touchscreen either, you bring your own phone, and the windows are hand cranks on plain steel wheels. The whole pitch is that keeping it this simple is what keeps it cheap, and that you finish the truck however you want from a catalog of more than 100 accessories.

The number moved

Why "under $20k" quietly became "mid-$20k"

The headline that pulled in 160,000 reservations leaned on a $7,500 federal EV tax credit. That credit ended, so the math changed. Slate now guides to a starting price in the mid-$20,000s, with a target around $27,500 before any accessories.

Still cheap for a new EV, and still well under an entry-level gas pickup. Just know the truck you reserved on a sub-$20k dream is now a high-$20k truck, and the final figure lands on June 24.

How we got here

The story so far

2022

Born inside Re:Build Manufacturing

Started as a project called "Re:Car" by Miles Arnone, William Barker and Jeff Wilke, the ex-Amazon consumer boss. Spun out as its own company in 2023.

Apr 24, 2025

The big reveal in Los Angeles

After a teaser stunt with wrapped prototypes, Slate showed the truck and the under-$20k-with-credit story. Reservations took off.

Mar 9, 2026

New CEO, months before launch

Christine Barman, the Chrysler veteran who had been the face of the company, handed the CEO seat to ex-Amazon executive Peter Faricy and moved to President of Vehicles.

Apr 2026

$650M Series C

TWG Global led another round, bringing total money raised to about $1.4 billion. The CEO said it funds production "on time and on budget."

May 2026

Bezos's board rep steps off

The Bezos family office representative left the board. Bezos has been pouring attention into a new robotics venture, which raised questions about how engaged he still is here.

Jun 24, 2026

Price reveal and preorders open

Final pricing announced. Reservations convert to non-refundable $300 preorders. This is your decision point.

Late 2026, into 2027

Production and first deliveries

Trucks are meant to start coming off the Indiana line in Q4 2026. Some reporting suggests newer reservation holders may not see one until 2027.

The scale

This is not two people in a garage

The honest scorecard

Green flags and red flags

Reasons for confidence

  • $1.4 billion raised and serious backers, including Bezos and TWG Global.
  • An actual factory being retrofitted, not just renderings.
  • 160,000+ reservations show real demand at this price.
  • A deal with Carvana to help handle sales and delivery.
  • Leadership keeps saying late-2026 production is on track.

Reasons for caution

  • They have never built a car before. First vehicles from startups slip.
  • The price already rose once, from under $20k to the mid-$20k range.
  • CEO swapped out months before launch.
  • Bezos's board representative left, and his focus seems to be elsewhere.
  • You commit a non-refundable $300 before a single truck has shipped.

Bottom line

How I would think about it

The simplest read: your $50 is doing real work right now, holding a priority slot at zero risk. The only thing June 24 forces is whether you want to pay $250 more to keep that slot once the deposit goes non-refundable.

If you can comfortably lose $300 and you actually want this exact truck, converting buys you an early delivery slot and locks in your spot in line. If any of the flags bother you, there is a clean middle path: wait, let the price land on the 24th, let the first trucks ship and get reviewed, and buy then. You give up early-slot priority, not the truck itself. Given that this is a company's first-ever vehicle and the deposit is non-refundable, waiting is the lower-regret move unless you are genuinely excited to be an early adopter.
