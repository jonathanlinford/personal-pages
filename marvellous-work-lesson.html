Come, Follow Me · September 21–27, 2026

A marvellous work
and a wonder.

Isaiah sees a dry wilderness bloom, tears wiped away, and truths brought back. God can restore what looks lost.

Isaiah 13–14; 22; 24–30; 35   /   Sunday, September 27   /   Primary, age 9   /   30 minutes

Jesus Christ can shelter me, save me, and restore what is lost or broken.

01

Remember Isaiah?

3 minutes

Open with prayer. Let the children answer before tapping.

Two quick callbacks

Which name did Isaiah give Jesus?

Though our sins are scarlet, Jesus can make them…

Last week Isaiah told us who Jesus is. This week he shows us what Jesus can do.

02

From desert to garden.

4 minutes

Ask everyone to make the driest, thirstiest pose they can. Isaiah describes a world that feels empty and broken—then the Lord changes it.

“The parched ground shall become a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water.”Isaiah 35:6–7
Dry ground
nothing seems able to grow
→
Living water
the wilderness blossoms
Ask the class

Can you think of something that seemed hopeless but became better with help, patience, or prayer?

Restore means to bring something back—to heal, repair, or return what was lost. That is today’s word.

03

“A refuge from the storm.”

5 minutes

Make a rainstorm together: rub hands for wind, pat knees for rain, then clap once for thunder. Freeze and listen.

“Thou hast been … a refuge from the storm, a shadow from the heat.”Isaiah 25:4

A refuge is a safe place. Jesus does not promise that no storm will ever come. He promises that we do not have to face storms alone.

Choose a refuge

What can help when your heart feels stormy?

Choose one, then explain how it can become a refuge.

04

He will wipe away tears.

4 minutes

Isaiah’s greatest promise is not only that Jesus helps us through hard things. Because Jesus died and rose again, death itself will not win.

“He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord God will wipe away tears from off all faces.”Isaiah 25:8–9
Isaiah’s promise
😢

Ask first: Does this verse mean we never feel sad?

Keep it clear

We still cry when someone dies. Jesus cried too. But His Resurrection means separation and death are not forever. That is why Isaiah says, “We will be glad and rejoice in his salvation.”

Say it together: “Because Jesus lives, death is not the end.”

05

What does it mean to restore?

4 minutes

Hold your hands apart like a broken object. As the class names each step, bring them closer: notice what is wrong, find the missing part, repair it, use it again.

Restore is more than replace.The Lord does not throw people away when they are hurt or when they make mistakes.
Restoration takes love and work.Jesus heals, forgives, teaches, and patiently helps us become whole.
Try one example

A friendship has been hurt by an unkind joke. What might “restoring” it look like? Tell the truth, apologize, repair what you can, and choose differently.

06

The gospel was restored.

8 minutes
“I will proceed to do a marvellous work among this people, even a marvellous work and a wonder.”Isaiah 29:14

After Jesus’s Apostles died, important authority and truths were lost from the earth. Heavenly Father did not forget His children. Through Joseph Smith, He began bringing Christ’s gospel back.

Watch the 5-minute Church video. Afterward ask: What did Heavenly Father bring back because He loves His children?

Tap each object

What did the Lord bring back?

Ask: Why is “marvellous” a good word for the Restoration?

The Restoration is not mainly about old things or buildings. It gives us Jesus Christ’s truth, covenants, authority, and power today.

07

One hopeful sentence.

2 minutes

Choose two or three names. Anyone may pass.

Choose a sentence starter.
“Behold, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us.”Isaiah 25:9
This week

Notice one thing God is restoring.

It might be a friendship, courage after fear, peace after prayer, or truth learned from the scriptures. Thank Him for it.

Share a brief testimony that Jesus Christ can shelter, save, and restore. Close with prayer.

Teacher notes, sources & timing

The seven steps total 30 minutes, including opening and closing prayers. If discussion runs long, use the video instead of the tap-each-object activity. No printing, purchases, or supplies are needed.

  • Core path: review 3, desert-to-garden 4, refuge 5, Resurrection 4, restore 4, Restoration video and discussion 8, closing 2.
  • Keep death discussion simple and hopeful. Do not ask children to share private grief.
  • The class pictures are newly generated, imagined examples based on the children’s reference images—not claims about events in their lives.
  • The official children’s outline suggests the shelter comparison, wiping away tears, and identifying latter-day works such as the Book of Mormon, temples, and the First Vision.
